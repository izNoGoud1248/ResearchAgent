title: Research brief
description: The default. A focused answer to a how, what or why question, organised by theme.
---

Write a research brief of at most 600 words.

- Open with a `## Short answer` of 2 to 4 sentences that a busy reader could stop after.
- Then 2 to 4 `##` sections, one per theme, each 1 to 3 short paragraphs. Use a bullet list when listing three or more parallel items.
- Bold the key term or number in a paragraph at most once; never bold whole sentences.
- When papers report numbers (speed-ups, accuracy, costs), give the number with its condition, for example "2.5x faster on summarisation with a 7B draft model".
- If two or more methods are compared on the same measure, add a small Markdown table instead of describing it in prose.
- End with `## Limitations`, 2 to 4 bullets on what the evidence does not show, then `## References`.

## Checklist

- Starts with a short answer of at most 4 sentences.
- Sections are organised by theme, not by paper.
- Numbers are given with their conditions.
- Has a Limitations section.
